About Me

I am a computational physicist based in Utrecht, Netherlands, currently pursuing my PhD at Utrecht University.

 Background

I was born and raised in Gallipoli, Italy. My love for math has been a constant in my life, as far back as I can remember. With the encouragement of my teachers, parents, and friends, my interest in science blossomed, particularly in theoretical physics, chemistry, and sustainable science.

In high school, I dove into various science projects and even won a chemistry olympics for green science with two of my peers. This early success fueled my passion and set me on a path to Bologna, where I pursued theoretical physics. Both my bachelor’s and master’s theses were centered on General Relativity. During my master’s, I had an incredible opportunity to spend three months at the Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics, finalizing my thesis project. After completing my master’s, I decided to switch fields. I moved to Utrecht to become a PhD candidate under the supervision of Zeila Zanolli. In our group, we explore the electronic and optical properties of materials for the quantum era, using first-principles methods. We push the boundaries of research by developing new theories and code. My current focus is on theoretical spectroscopy and superconductivity.

My time at Utrecht University has been transformative, both professionally and personally. I’ve gained immense knowledge, expanded my academic horizons, and built a strong network of colleagues and friends. It’s been an enriching experience that I am truly grateful for.

When I’m not immersed in research, I love staying active. Running and working out are my go-to activities. I also enjoy playing video games.
